# LOD that coarsens with camera speed

The distance `d0` at which a level-0 Voxel covers one pixel is the LOD rule's only size (ADR 0002). It now shrinks as the camera moves faster: full detail up to 10 m/s, then a ramp to about three levels coarser at 50 m/s for the levels from 0 to 4 and one and a half for the levels past them (the first curve was one and a half for all, and its flights at 2 m were still short of level 0 bricks, #168). A fast camera draws coarser bricks on purpose. It was decided in the plan review of 2026-11-10 and amends ADR 0002's "one Voxel never covers more than about a pixel", which now holds for a slow camera.

We chose it because the strict rule cannot be met at 5 cm. Flying at 50 m/s at full detail needs 1.07–1.29 million generated bricks a second, and eight cores generate about 150 thousand: 7 to 8 times short. The acceptance "terrain keeps up with 50 m/s flight" was therefore failing by construction, as a stream of soft misses that no amount of prefetch fixes. Each level coarser needs about a quarter of the bricks for the same ground, so one and a half levels was taken to be about the 8× that is missing, and at speed the lost detail is under the motion on screen. **That count was of the bricks in a shell, which is not what a flight makes**: it makes new ground, a strip as wide as the shell, at the speed of the camera, and the strip's width is in proportion to the scale of `d0`, not its square (reasoning, not yet measured: the per-path generated and arrived counts and the loader's queue that the real-time bench now reports are the measurement). At that rate one and a half levels (a scale of 0.35) is about 2.8× fewer bricks a second, not 8×, which leaves the flights short of what the generator makes by about 2.5 to 3× at 50 m/s.

- **It is the drawn rule, not a fallback.** The band selects the coarser level, the shell holds it, and the prefetcher asks for it. A brick drawn at the speed-scaled level is not a soft miss (`CONTEXT.md`).
- **It coarsens at once and refines when the camera slows.** Full detail returns within a settle time after the camera slows or stops; the time is measured by the streaming bench, reported first, and gated once known.
- **The curve's numbers come from the streaming bench**, not from this ADR: the speeds, the depth of the ramp and any hysteresis are set so that the bench's paths show no soft miss at the speed-scaled level.
- **The Zoom uses the same rule**, with the camera's speed along the Zoom.
- **The spawn uses the same idea in distance:** play starts when a 20 m ring is at full detail (≤ ~2 s), and loading goes on nearest first, to 40 m and then the shell.

The acceptance becomes: no holes, ever; zero soft misses at the speed-scaled level on the bench's paths; frame time p99 ≤ 16.7 ms and frame-thread CPU p99 ≤ 8 ms, on the full-size Planet on the 4090.

## Considered Options

- **Keep the strict rule and make generation 8× faster:** GPU generation or a much cheaper generator. A research project with no known end, and bit-identical generation (ADR 0003) rules out the easy GPU path.
- **Keep the strict rule and accept soft misses at speed:** the same picture, but unplanned: the level drawn depends on what happened to arrive, so it pops, and "soft miss" stops being a bug that a gate can catch.
- **Lower the flight speed:** 50 m/s is already slow for a 320 km Planet (ADR 0008).
- **A coarser level only in the direction of travel:** the turn is the hard case (ADR 0004); a view-dependent rule brings the frustum problem back.

## Consequences

- `Bands::d0` becomes a function of camera speed, on the client only; the server and the generator do not know it.
- The shell at speed is smaller than the shell at rest, so memory is bounded by the camera at rest.
- Detail visibly sharpens after a stop. The settle time is the number the player feels, which is why it is gated.
- The streaming bench must exist before the curve can be set: it is the first M1a-2 ticket.

## The curve, as built (#168)

- **Depth** is 0 up to 10 m/s and a linear ramp to 3 levels at 50 m/s and above, taken in steps of half a level (0, 0.5 ... 3): `d0` is scaled by 2^-depth for the levels 0 to 4 (`render::lod::NEAR_LEVELS`), and by no less than 2^-1.5 (`FAR_DEPTH`) for the levels past them, so the far levels are drawn as the first curve drew them and the bands stay in order (`render::lod::Bands::speed_scaled`, `SpeedScale`). The near levels are where a flight's new ground is made in bricks, and a level of coarser bands is about a quarter of the bricks for the same ground; at 50 m/s level 0 reaches 13 m from the camera, level 1 26 m, level 2 51 m, level 3 103 m. (Decision A of the owner, 2026-11-16: "about 3 levels at 50 m/s, on the near levels only", after the 4090 showed the 2 m flight's misses to be the loader's.) A step is taken at once, rounded up, when the speed asks for it, and given back only when 1.1 times the speed no longer asks for it, so noise about a step's speed flips nothing; the selection's test holds a flight at a steady speed (with 4% noise on it) to the same drawn set, frame by frame, as the exact speed gives, and to one change of bands.
- **Both are in `Bands`, and the coarser wins.** The memory cap's coarsening (ADR 0004) is applied to the speed-scaled bands (`Engine::bands_at`): a level's floor is `d0 · 2^L · min(scale, 1/factor)`, so where both apply the coarser of the two is used and they do **not** compound (the first version divided the scaled floor by the factor again, which at 1.5 levels with the cap's 1.5 was 4 times coarser than either asked for); the order of the bands holds. `Bands::d0` is the base `d0`, unscaled: the jump (`JUMP × d0`), the look ahead and the reserve are in the units of the scene at rest. The follow limit's least cost per Voxel is scaled by the bands' scale (`Engine::follow_limit`: a Voxel of a coarser scene costs about that much less, so a step may be longer), where the first version left it at 64 Voxels at any depth.
- **The speed is the bands' own input.** The scene thread is given the camera's speed separately from the prefetch velocity (`SceneWorker::follow_at`, `World::recentre_at`): a Zoom (#58) faster than the lead is cut at (`MOST_LEAD_SPEED`), or a lead that is off (`World::set_lead(false)`), is still drawn at the depth its speed asks. A single sample of zero (the velocity is reset on a frame over a quarter of a second) gives back nothing: **a step is given back only after the speed has been under its release point for half a second** (`RELEASE_HOLD`), timed from a timestamp: `SpeedScale::observe(speed, now)` records when the speed first fell under, and a sample above the release point starts it again (the first version added up the time that each look saw, capped, which a busy thread made too short). **A teleport is not a speed**: a camera that is put further than a jump has speed 0 for that frame (`Motion`), the depth is its speed's step at once with any pending release ended (`SpeedScale::settle_to`), and the scene is made again at the camera with those bands (`Scene::jump_to`) with nothing listed to look at again: the release used to come half a second after the jump and list the whole of the new scene.
- **A change of depth is made in place**, not by making the scene again (which is 10 s at full size), and **in its own budgeted calls**, not as part of a follow step. `Selection::set_bands` only lists the nodes to look at (a coarser step: the split nodes, coarsest first, since a merge takes what is under it, and **not** the whole nodes that hold a margin, which come due early by their own entry; a finer step: the whole nodes of level 1 and up, nearest the camera first), takes them out of the way of `update` (a node that is listed is parked until it is looked at, so that an update does not change them all at once as if the camera had moved: that was a 7 s follow step of 3.3 million bricks), and keeps what a change before it had not looked at. **Every listed node is parked, for a coarser step too**: parking only the ones on the hot list (so that a queued split node merged late by its own entry) was tried and failed: at a flight's speed the camera is past the old slack of nearly all of them in a round or two, and they merge in one update, outside the budget (`docs/measurements.md`, "Why the stop's peak is 780 to 796 K of level 0"). `Selection::relook_some` looks at some of them, and `Engine::reband_more` calls it once a round, after following the camera, checked against the upload budget and sized from the cost of the last call as a follow step is. A follow step is sized from its own change only, and what the bands' change costs is never charged to it (the first version charged it, which shrank follow steps to `FOLLOW_STEP_LEAST`). The scene is not settled (`Scene::waiting` counts them) until all are looked at. The nodes' old entries are not lost to a change that goes back: a node's stamp is kept under a bit while it is parked, and a change **back to the bands that the parked nodes' entries were made for** (a depth given back before the nodes it listed are looked at) restores them instead of looking at them (an update that finds a parked node's entry due drops it, and that node is looked at after all). The stale entries that looking leaves are dropped when the last node is looked at, or when the lists come to more than twice the nodes, so that bands that go to and fro do not grow them. The time of the change is reported apart from following: `reband_ms` in the scene thread's parts.
- **The numbers come from the full-size real-time bench with no GPU** on the 8-core dev box (`a_full_size_run_with_no_gpu_reports_what_the_scene_thread_did`; the scene thread's counts are as the 4090's, the times are not; the box is shared and its load was 5 to 15): `docs/measurements.md`, "Speed-scaled LOD". **With the camera followed (the drawn scene is at most 1.0 `d0` behind it) there are still soft misses on the flights at 2 m and 50 m: 480 and 275 frames of about 590 (the scene's own count), 383 and 249 under the camera (a ray down from the frame's camera)**, against 482 and 419 with no speed scaling on a quiet box. The zeros of the first version were the lag: a scene that is 9 `d0` behind the camera has already been generated for the ground it is on, and is counted against its own centre. **The earlier reading of these misses as the lag's is withdrawn** (it was that level 0 is within 0.71 `d0` of the scene's centre at 1.5 levels while the scene is 0.55 to 1.0 `d0` behind the camera): the re-review at `0bbb089` measured no misses that come from the lag, scored at the frame's camera. They are on the scene's side: bricks that the scene's own bands ask for and the loader has not made. **The generator is not what limits the loader: the scene thread's taking in is** (measured, #168, with counters in the loader's workers): in a flight the workers spent 82 to 87% of their time waiting with jobs queued, because they are never more than `MAX_AHEAD` (512) jobs ahead of what the scene thread has taken in, and 3 to 7% generating; `Engine::stream` ran out its 2 ms `SLICE` on 419 of 420 calls, and a flight's round is about 24 ms (follow about 10, `reband_more` about 8), so taking in had 7% of the thread and took in 920 jobs' children a second against 9 600 when it has the thread to itself (the cold start). The 4090's 4.3 to 7.8 thousand bricks a second in flights, against 26 to 40 thousand in the stop and the cold start, is the same limit. The rate argument above (a flight needs about 2.8× fewer bricks a second at 1.5 levels) is untested by this and not contradicted: the loader never got to make them. **The first fix is a longer slice while the loader is backed up** (`BACKED_UP_SLICE`, 32 ms, only while `Loader::backed_up`, cut short when the frame thread has given the thread a job, an Edit's among them): a flight takes in 4 to 9 times as many bricks (159 to 177 thousand at 2 m in 10 s, 120 to 140 thousand at 50 m, against 41 and 55 thousand), the loader's queue mean falls from 41 to about 25 to 40 thousand at 2 m and from 13 to 9 thousand at 50 m, and the camera's soft-miss frames from 387 and 286 to 287 to 404 and 0. What is left at 2 m is the loader still behind (`children_resident` false, requested) with the scene thread full: follow 4.8 s, taking in 2.6 s, reband 1 to 1.5 s of 10; its count moves by hundreds of frames with the box's load (63 to 404 over runs of a quiet box and not), so whether it reaches 0 is for the 4090. A slice of 64 ms was not read: the box was at a load of 18 to 31. `client bench --stream-slice-ms` measures other slices (16, 32 and 64: `docs/measurements.md`).
- **The 45 frames of soft misses at frames 245 to 289 of the 50 m flight were margin nodes parked by a change to coarser bands** (found by logging the node under the camera at each miss, #168): 59 of the 71 misses were a whole node with its margin held and its children resident, in the relook's list (`Selection::set_bands` listed the whole nodes with a margin as well as the split ones, "which may let go the margin") and so parked, watched by nothing, with the relook 590 thousand nodes behind. A parked node cannot split. A margin node need not be listed for coarser bands: its entry is for a split nearer than the old bands', which is further than the new ones', so it comes due early and the node is watched again then; the margin that it may now let go is let go then, a little late, which costs nothing. It is not listed now (`a_margin_node_is_split_by_a_camera_that_comes_to_it_before_a_coarser_change_is_looked_at`), and the 50 m flight has 0 frames of soft misses under the camera in 5 runs at a dev-box load of 8 to 13 (it had 45 in each of 3 before, and in an A/B run with the margin nodes listed again); in two runs at a load of 15 and 31 it had 14 and 451, with the scene thread too slow to follow. **The finer-bands direction has the same shape and is not fixed**: a whole node's entry is for a split further in than the new bands', so it comes due late, and the relook that is behind is all there is; a flight's depth only ever goes finer when the speed falls.
- **Not met on the dev box**: the flights at 2 m (about 290 to 400 frames under the camera, the loader behind with the scene thread full) and, for the settle, the relook (see below).
- **The gates** (`bench-budgets.toml`): soft misses **under the camera** (a ray down from the frame's camera: the level drawn there against the level the speed-scaled bands ask at that distance, less the hysteresis) on the four flights, zero; the drawn scene's lag in `d0` on the flights, 8.0 for now (main's 7.81 on the 4090 plus a margin) and **to tighten to about 2 once the follow fixes land**; the settle time, 55 s (main's 47.8 s on the 4090 plus 15%), where the scene is also to be at the camera; the spawn ring, 2 s; and the real time from the world map being ready to the window opening, 10 s. The scene thread's own soft-miss count (against the scene's lagging camera) is reported, not gated.
- **A release that would list more than 500 thousand nodes makes the scene again** (`Rebuild::Release`, the owner's fallback B: "if the stop still relooks more than about 500 K nodes, or settle > 55 s, do one full rebuild"). A finer step at a flight's end lists 1.2 to 4.2 million nodes at full size, which the relook takes 40 s over and keeps the scene finer than it ends up for; `Engine::rescale` counts what the listing came to and, over `RELEASE_REBUILD_OVER`, makes the scene again at the new depth at the scene's camera (`Scene::rebuild_for`), which drops the stale set with the old selection. It is one update (`Rebuilt`) that the frame thread copies in full like the jump's (over budget, counted under `over_budget.bands`), and `World::reband_pending` stays true until it is published (the stats and the new scene are one publish). What it costs the scene thread is `scene_thread.release_ms` and `scene_release_rebuilds` in the report, against #217's 5 s `CATCH_UP_CAP`, which is not changed. The rebuilt scene has the resident bricks only: the loader makes the finer bricks round the stop after it (`fill_s`), where the relook made them in step with it.
- **A Still view holds the reband too** (#217 on #168). The scene thread holds the scene while the view is Still (ADR 0005): a patch of a reband restarts the Reference view like a follow step. So `Engine::rescale` does not look at the speed and `reband_more` does not run while the scene is held; leaving Still does both and catches up. The catch-up before the hold waits for it: `World::reband_pending` (nodes listed and not yet looked at, or a step of the depth still to be given back, which the scene thread publishes as `Stats::speed_releasing`) is part of `World::caught_up`, inside #217's 5 s cap, which the owner kept (2026-11-16). A reband of a flight is 50 s or more at full size on the dev box, so the cap is expected to end that wait after a flight at speed: `still_after_flight.before_hold.hit_cap` is what says so, and what the cap held is reported, not hidden. Every patch is tagged `Cause::Reband`.
- **Settle after a stop from 50 m/s** is the finer bands' bricks being made (a coarse scene made fine again: 4 to 5 million bricks, 25 to 50 s of the scene thread on the dev box, nearest the camera first) and the loader generating the ground round the stop that the finer level asks for, which is the part of it that waits for the loader. Reported in three parts, `walk_s` (the scene reaching where the camera stopped: under a second now), `reband_s` (until every node of the change for the stop is looked at) and `fill_s` (the loader's bricks, and making them into the scene). The bench catch-up between paths waits for the scene to be at the camera as well as for the loader (it ended on the stats of the path before, which left the scene up where the turn's predecessor had put it). It was 102 to 115 s on the dev box at a load of 5 to 15, against 52 s with no speed scaling on a quiet box; with the longer slice the loader keeps up in the stop and `fill_s` is 0 to 10 s, and the settle is the relook's, 53 to 57 s (`reband_s` 50 to 57, one run at 120 s with the box at load 30 and others' tests running). The first version's explanation, that there is "more ground at the finer level", is withdrawn: the code does not support it (the finer scene has no more ground than the coarse one, only more bricks over it).
- **The spawn ring is 20 m** (`SPAWN_REACH`): 0.83 to 0.88 s at full size on the dev box (40 m was 3.4 s). The Guard that follows it in the load takes 4 s, and the window waits for it, so that nothing past the ring is sky; the bench reports the two (`spawn_ring_s`, `guard_s`) and gates the ring, and the real time to the window (`time_to_control_s`: ring and Guard, 10 s). **"Control in 2 s with nothing beyond the ring sky" is not met**: it is #220, a background Guard.
