# The scene follows the camera on a thread of its own, over a shared store

Choosing which bricks are drawn and packing them for the GPU ran on the frame thread, and cost it from a millisecond to tens of them a frame: the selection decides which bricks change level as the camera moves, each region that gains or loses one is packed again, and taking in the bricks the loader makes does the same work. The frame thread now does none of it. A **scene thread** owns the scene of rings (the selection, the packed words, the free space) and the loader, follows the latest camera it was told, takes in arrivals, and publishes what it made. The frame thread takes the newest result and applies it: it writes the words that changed into its own copy of the scene (the one that is traced on the CPU to aim, and copied to the GPU) and nothing else. The selection and the packing of a region's bricks are the code that ran on the frame thread, except that `repack_region` was rewritten for the layout below.

The store is shared, not copied. The client's predictor holds it behind a read-write lock, and the scene thread reads it to select and pack and writes to it for two things: to hold what the loader generated (bricks that were not held; nothing in the type enforces that), and, since #156, to remake the coarse levels of the pyramid over the bricks an Edit changed, which only this thread reads (the frame thread, the character and the ghost read level 0 and nothing else). The Edit's Voxels, level 0, are written through the predictor on the frame thread, as is the server's word. The frame thread owns the Edit's history and the character's view of the ground; the scene thread owns the scene and the loader.

The measured times and sizes behind this ADR are in [`docs/measurements.md`](../measurements.md#the-scene-thread-adr-0006).

- **The GPU never sees a half-applied state.** A result is the scene after the thread finished a job, with the words copied out of it. A result the frame thread has not taken when the next is made is merged with it in the outbox, under the lock the frame thread takes with, and is never taken out: the words are copied again from the scene as it is then, so taking one is the same as taking both. A copy of all of the scene that is waiting (the scene was packed again: a jump, other bands) has later changes pasted into it, not made again. A buffer or table that runs out of room is not packed again: it grows alone, and the patch that needs the room carries the new lengths (#145).
- **Uploads are bounded by what the scene thread adds, not by what a frame takes** (#145). Cutting a patch at the frame thread would let a frame draw a brick whose words are not on the GPU. So the scene thread holds work back instead: while what it has published and not yet had taken, plus what it has changed and not yet published, costs more than a budget (bytes and `write_buffer` calls, `scene_worker::UPLOAD_BUDGET`), it stops following the camera and taking in arrivals and carries them to later rounds, in the order they came. Every patch is whole, so the frame draws only what it has the words of. An Edit's jobs are not held back, so the bound below holds; nor is a jump or other bands, which replace the scene.
- **Edits show the frame after they are made** (#156). The frame thread changes level 0 of the store and hands the bricks changed to the scene thread, which packs them at once, with the coarse levels as they were (near the player everything drawn is level 0), ahead of the camera and the arrivals, and publishes. It then remakes the coarse levels over them (most of what the Edit costs, so the predictor leaves it: `Predictor::defer_lod`) in short steps, holding the store's write lock for one step at a time (`BrickStore::step_lod`) and letting go between them, and packs the bricks over them again when they are made. **The bound:** the frame thread looks at the scene once the frame the Edit is made, and the next frame it takes what the thread has published, waiting for the level-0 pack for `EDIT_WAIT` at the most. So an Edit shows in the frame after the one that made it if the scene thread has packed it by then (C1's bound in `docs/research/scheduling-and-upload.md`), which it has unless it was busy for most of a frame with the camera or the arrivals; and else in the first look after it has. Two Edits in two frames: the older one's frame decides. The coarse levels are briefly as they were, which draws the old state of far bricks, never a hole. A rejection that undoes an Edit, or the server's word that changes the Voxels, is still waited for. The result stays in the outbox until the frame thread takes it, so an Edit, a rejection that undoes one, or other bands are never taken empty.
- **The frame thread waits for the scene thread only for those jobs.** It tells it the camera and takes what is ready with try-locks (a result being handed over is there the next frame). It does not free what the scene thread allocated: copies and the old copy of the scene go back through a channel, because memory that one thread allocates and another frees can make the second wait in the allocator for as long as the first spends making a big table, which was the worst frame of the first version. If the scene thread panics the frame thread panics (a guard wakes it), and the panic is reported when the thread is joined.
- **What is drawn is the same.** The cut through the pyramid is whole at every moment, so a camera that is followed late is a coarser or finer frame, never a hole.

## Considered Options

- **A smaller change on the frame thread** (cheaper repacking, batching): walking gets close, flying does not.
- **The selection on a thread, the packing on the frame thread:** the packing needs the selection (what is drawn, the holes round the camera) and so would need it on both, and an Edit would round-trip through the thread anyway.
- **A copy of the store for the scene thread:** hundreds of megabytes of bricks, and every arrival and Edit sent twice. The lock costs a wait only when the frame thread writes.
- **Aiming against the store instead of keeping a copy of the scene on the frame thread:** would save the second copy (about 542 MiB at full size), but the CPU tracer (`render::reference`) walks the packed scene, the GPU upload reads the same copy, and a ray through the store would hit what the store says rather than what is drawn (a coarser brick, a brick not yet split). Not done.

## Consequences

- **Peak host memory** is the scene thread's buffers, the frame thread's copy (the same size, written in full when it is made so that pasting never waits for a page), and, while the scene is packed again, one more copy of all of it waiting in the outbox and the old one on its way back: up to about four times the scene's reserve for the moment of a jump or other bands. A scene that runs out of room grows the buffer that filled, with slack, and no more than that (#145).
- The frame thread's work on the scene is a memory copy of the changes and the upload, whatever the camera does. What the camera does costs the scene thread, which may follow it late when it moves fast: the Planet then draws what the camera was a few frames ago (the full-size test reports how far behind the rings are).
- Filling the Planet no longer waits for frames: the scene thread takes in arrivals as fast as the loader makes them. It sleeps until told when nothing is on its way, and polls every millisecond while the loader has bricks coming.
- **Stalls that remain.** The frame thread's store writes (an Edit, or the server's word that changes bricks) wait for the scene thread's read, which lasts the whole of following the camera, and for the packing of an Edit. Its reads wait for no more than a step of the coarse levels being remade, the one time the scene thread holds the write lock for more than an arrival. A server message that changes bricks (a reject, in multiplayer other players' Edits) or a change of bands still waits on a round trip to the scene thread, so a stream of them takes frames from the frame thread; an Edit of the player's own does not. The ghost made again after a click is planned and outlined on the frame thread, brick by brick from the masks, and backs off; it does not wait on the scene thread. A click at a cold spot, where the plan reads bricks the generator has to make for the first time, costs more than half a frame; that is accepted. The frame path has locks (the store's, the outbox's): the research doc's "no locks on the frame path" is the target, and this is where it is not. The std lock favours a waiting writer on Linux, but a Windows slim reader-writer lock makes no such promise. Releasing the read between the selection and the packing would cut the wait to the longer of the two, and between batches of regions to about a millisecond; that is for when it matters.
- The words of a region's mixed bricks are packed together, in the order of its mask, so that the GPU buffer's layout does not depend on the order the camera brought the bricks in (the reference's incoherent rays read it better the more of a region is in one place). A change that adds or resizes a mixed brick writes the region's bricks again; a change that only drops bricks or adds solid ones writes the region's list of entries, and the rest stays. This writes about three times as much to the GPU (#163 removes it).
