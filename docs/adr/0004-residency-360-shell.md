# Residency is a complete 360° shell with an always-resident guard

The client keeps a complete shell of LOD rings around the camera, in every azimuth, rather than a frustum-shaped set in front of it. The view direction orders work and sets refinement priority; it does not decide what is resident. A coarse guard (levels 9–13 at 5 cm Voxels, 74 MiB) is always resident, so a late fine brick becomes a coarser frame rather than a hole.

We chose this because turning, not flying, is the hard case. A 180°/s turn sweeps the horizon at about 800 m/s, sixteen times the 50 m/s flight speed. A frustum-shaped resident set would need a 104°-wide region in 0.58 s, which no prefetcher can guarantee; without a guard it shows holes, and with one it shows visible coarse pops. The shell is about 227–259 MB per scene copy near the ground (the prefetch simulator at 5 cm, #49), small against the ~2 GB budget, and the guard is 74 MiB and generates from the seed in about 0.8 s on eight cores.

## Considered Options

- **Frustum-only residency with a prefetch cone:** saves 2–3× memory and generation, but only works for slow turns. Rejected for the shipped path; kept as a debug mode.
- **Frustum plus the guard, no shell:** hides holes but not coarse pops, and the pop is exactly what the fidelity bar forbids.
- **Pre-warming the whole Planet:** 327 MB at levels 6–13; a hundred times the live set and mostly below the horizon. Rejected.
- **A hard 2 GB cap that drops required bricks:** turns a budget into a hole. Exceed the budget by a bounded overflow, then coarsen the LOD.

## Consequences

- The resident set is view-independent; the view could order refinement, and the loader does not use it yet (it works by class and distance).
- A 1.25× radial margin and a 20 m vertical margin are residency, not prefetch (#143). Both are measured on the distance the bands are applied to: a brick drawn whole has its eight children resident when it is nearer than 1.25 times the distance the bands split it at (so 25% of each band's start, 26 m before level 1's), or when a camera raised 20 m along the Planet's radial up would be (`render::lod::Reach`). The children are not drawn: the bricks drawn are the bands'. The margin is one level under what is drawn, since a child is only a margin brick of its own while it is nearer than 1.25 times its own, finer, boundary. The client has no horizon cap yet (it draws and so holds the whole Planet, see below), so the margin is a lead on the band boundaries and not on the horizon, and a vertical take-off, which the simulator needs 1.75× for against a horizon cap, reveals nothing in the client. Measured in the full-size client the margins cost 30% more of the store (20.90 million counted against 16.13 million; 3.15 million units resident against 2.18 million), 45% more bricks generated (13.67 million against 9.41 million) and a fill of 133–150 s against 98 s, and draw 0.7% fewer bricks (3.416 million against 3.440 million: the bricks they make resident are known to be full beside the drawn ones, which buries a few more). 1.25 stays (the user's call, #143): it is the ADR's value, and the measurements below do not move it. It is a lead on the band boundaries, not a bound: the flights miss because generation is slower than the camera, whatever the margin is.
- **Prefetch** (#143) looks 1 s and 2 s along the camera's velocity (a running mean of the last tenth of a second of the frames, nothing below 1 m/s), at the lowest priority, and is never relied on: the bands do not wait for it and eviction does not keep it. It is the same walk as the margin from the places ahead instead of the camera, with the bricks below the ones drawn there walked down to the finest level the bands will want (the camera will not have split those bricks when it gets there). A lead that has moved by under a sixteenth of `d0` is kept, so noise in the velocity does not make the selection look again.
- **The loader works in classes** (B1 of the scheduling research): the bricks the bands split first (they are drawn coarser until their children arrive: a soft miss), then the margin, then prefetch, and by distance from the camera in each. The view direction orders nothing: the loader is not told it, and the selection does not look at it, so a turn needs nothing the scene did not already have. Work that nothing wants any more is dropped when a worker comes to it, as before.
- **Soft misses in the client** (#143), counted by the scene thread as the bricks drawn whole although the bands split them, at 60 frames a second in real time on the 8-core dev box, the shell complete and the camera still at the start of each path (`soft_misses_on_the_flight_take_off_and_standing_paths` in `streaming_tests.rs`). Frames with a soft miss, of the frames of the path, and the most bricks missing at once; the margins-only row is two runs and the margins-and-prefetch row three (the third, after the unlocked reorder of the loader's queue: 231 of 597, peak 69 K at 2 m; 166 of 597, peak 12.7 K at 50 m), and the counts move by a factor of two or more between runs (the loader's rate is what they measure), so read them as orders of magnitude:

  | Path | no margins, no prefetch | margins | margins and prefetch |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 50 m/s flight at 2 m, 10 s | 585 of 598; peak 165 K | 472–532 of 596; peak 78–105 K | 215–311 of 596; peak 45–70 K |
  | 50 m/s flight at 50 m, 10 s | 557 of 598; peak 148 K | 269–467 of 597; peak 12–54 K | 166–381 of 596; peak 2–13 K |
  | 50 m/s vertical take-off, 6 s | 0 of 359 | 0 of 359 | 0 of 358 |
  | standing still, 2 s | 0 of 120 | 0 of 120 | 0 of 120 |

  The flights are limited by generation, not by what is resident: with the loader at about 150 K bricks a second against the 1.1–1.3 million the simulator asks for (the first column's backlog is 435 K to 540 K bricks at the end of a flight), no residency margin prevents misses, it halves or thirds them and prefetch cuts them to a tenth where the lead lands on ground that the loader can still reach. The take-off and standing still miss nothing in any column, because the client draws the whole Planet and nothing it holds depends on the view or on altitude. The 180° turn criterion of the ticket does not apply to this client: the selection is view-independent (it looks at the camera's position and never at its direction) and the whole Planet is drawn, so a turn changes nothing that is wanted or resident, and the 2 s of standing still is all that it can be measured as; the horizon-cap case that makes turning the hard one (above) returns when a horizon cap does. Margins and prefetch stay within the budget (the budget is the complete set with the margins, 19.8 per d0 squared, and the flights stayed under its limit), and nothing took a step of coarsening.
- Eviction is distance-band first, then LRU inside the band, with 10–20% overflow and no eviction of the guard, the current frame's set, the margin, in-flight work or a live Edit chain (#141, #143). Prefetch units are not kept: they are candidates like any other, and go first when the store is over its budget since they were never stamped as wanted. It runs on the scene thread (ADR 0006), a part of the units at a time. At full size listing the units takes 12–15 s of the scene thread's time with the margins (the store passes the limit by 4% meanwhile in the 5 km flight), so the limit is tight. A later full-size run (after the loader's queue was reordered without its lock held) peaked at 22.22 million in the 5 km flight against a limit of 23.03 million, with 2.37 million bricks evicted in two passes, the scene 472 MiB after the fill and 220 MiB at the end of the flight, and the fill generating 13.67 million bricks in 154 s with the machine loaded: the reorder starves nobody.
- Two sizes are budgeted, and they are different quantities. The drawn scene is bounded by the bands. The prefetch simulator sizes its shell at 227–259 MB per scene copy near the ground (about 1.7–1.9 million bricks at 136 bytes); the client measures 474 MiB per scene copy there (3.44 million drawn bricks at 144.5 bytes). #143 found why they differ: the simulator's shell stops at the horizon (1.25 times the horizon), and the client has no horizon cap yet, so it draws the whole Planet, every level to the far side, which is twice the bricks at the ground. The simulator's whole-Planet count was right (3.25 d0 squared, the calibration test) and its bytes a brick a little low (136 against 144.5); `prefetch-sim` now prints the client's whole-Planet size beside the shell (`built_size`), and its intake (35.2 K drawn bricks a second) and generation cost (62 µs a generated brick) are the #142 fill's: 3.44 million drawn and 9.41 million generated in 97.8 s on six workers. The model generates 1.7 bricks per drawn brick and the client 2.7, so its generation columns undercount the client's by 1.6×. The full-size test holds the scene to 512 MiB. The host store holds the bricks the scene splits, their ancestors, the margin's children and what the bands do not draw, except the air and the buried bricks, which the loader does not generate (#142): 20.9 million counted bricks at the complete shell with the margins (19.8 per d0 squared; 16.1 million without them, 15.3 per d0 squared; 29.3 million before #142); the budget is that, with a 10% overflow before eviction starts. `STREAMED_PER_D0_SQUARED` was raised from #141's 15.3 to 19.8 to fit the margins (#143: 30% more of the store is resident with them), and that is paid for in the fill, which generates 45% more bricks and takes 133-150 s against 98 s.
- **At the cap, the LOD is coarsened** (#143), where #141's interim waited for the store to grow by a quarter: when eviction has taken what it can (everything left is wanted) and the store is over its limit, the farthest bands are drawn at a larger bias, a step at a time: levels 8 and up at a bias of 1.5, then 7 and up, 6, 5, then 4 and up at 2, then 2 and up at 2 (`Bands::coarsened`, `COARSENING` in `scene_worker`). A step selects and packs the scene again (as a change of bias does), at most one in 10 s, and the units the coarser bands do not want go to eviction. A step is taken back by what the scene wants, not by what the store holds (eviction stops once the store is under its budget, so the store never falls far below it, and a rule on its size would never take a step back): at each step the units the store held for a node of the selection are recorded (everything it held was wanted), and a step is taken back, once the last has stood for its hold and nothing is on its way, when the nodes the scene wants now, scaled by the bands' `Bands::weight` at the step before over that now (a step's bricks over flat ground, in d0 squared), and by that units-per-node, are under 95% of the budget. A step is taken at the limit (up to 10% over the budget), so there is a gap of 15% or more between taking a step and taking it back, as well as the hold, and the steps do not flip: a camera that slows or settles (the lead and the trail are not wanted, or it is where less is wanted) gives its steps back one at a time, and one that still wants as much keeps them. The scene thread parks while it waits for that, and wakes for a hold that is over or for the camera. Eviction that has nothing to take no longer backs off by a quarter of the store: it waits for the store to grow by a quarter of the overflow. On the test Planet a budget at 90% of the complete set took five steps and drew 9.4 thousand bricks instead of 21.0 thousand, so the steps are coarse and the budget should be sized so that the shell fits (the full-size client does not coarsen at rest or in a 5 km flight with the budget above).
- The shell is the precondition for the horizon cap and the LOD claims: culling without a complete shell is a hole, and LOD without prefetch is a pop.
- Most of the price is paid in M1a-2, not M1a-1: the walk slice ships a simpler ring loader, and the shell lands with flight and orbit. The guard is the exception (2026-10-31): the walk slice's loader (#86) generates it right after the spawn ring, so nothing beyond the spawn ring is ever sky.
