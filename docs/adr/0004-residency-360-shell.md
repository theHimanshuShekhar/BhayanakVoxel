# Residency is a complete 360° shell with an always-resident guard

The client keeps a complete shell of LOD rings around the camera, in every azimuth, rather than a frustum-shaped set in front of it. The view direction orders work and sets refinement priority; it does not decide what is resident. A coarse guard (levels 9–13 at 5 cm Voxels and a 16 km radius; about four levels coarser for the same bytes at 320 km, ADR 0008) is always resident, so a late fine brick becomes a coarser frame rather than a hole.

We chose this because turning, not flying, is the hard case: a fast turn sweeps the horizon many times faster than flight does, and no prefetcher can guarantee a frustum-shaped set in time. Without a guard that shows holes, and with one it shows visible coarse pops. The shell and the guard are small against the ~2 GB budget.

The measured sizes, costs and soft-miss counts behind this ADR are in [`docs/measurements.md`](../measurements.md#residency-and-the-shell-adr-0004). They were taken on the 16 km Planet at rest; ADR 0008 (320 km) and ADR 0009 (a smaller `d0` at speed) change them, and the streaming bench measures them again.

## Considered Options

- **Frustum-only residency with a prefetch cone:** saves 2–3× memory and generation, but only works for slow turns. Rejected for the shipped path; kept as a debug mode.
- **Frustum plus the guard, no shell:** hides holes but not coarse pops, and the pop is exactly what the fidelity bar forbids.
- **Pre-warming the whole Planet:** a hundred times the live set and mostly below the horizon. Rejected.
- **A hard 2 GB cap that drops required bricks:** turns a budget into a hole. Exceed the budget by a bounded overflow, then coarsen the LOD.

## Consequences

- The resident set is view-independent; the view could order refinement, and the loader does not use it yet (it works by class and distance).
- **Margins are residency, not prefetch** (#143): a 1.25× radial margin and a 20 m vertical margin. Both are measured on the distance the bands are applied to: a brick drawn whole has its eight children resident when it is nearer than 1.25 times the distance the bands split it at, or when a camera raised 20 m along the Planet's radial up would be (`render::lod::Reach`). The children are not drawn: the bricks drawn are the bands'. The margin is one level under what is drawn, since a child is only a margin brick of its own while it is nearer than 1.25 times its own, finer, boundary. 1.25 stays (the user's call, #143). It is a lead on the band boundaries, not a bound: the flights miss because generation is slower than the camera, whatever the margin is. With the horizon cap (#146, below) the margin is also a lead on the horizon.
- **Prefetch** (#143) looks 1 s and 2 s along the camera's velocity (a running mean of the last tenth of a second of the frames, nothing below 1 m/s), at the lowest priority, and is never relied on: the bands do not wait for it and eviction does not keep it. It is the same walk as the margin from the places ahead instead of the camera, with the bricks below the ones drawn there walked down to the finest level the bands will want (the camera will not have split those bricks when it gets there). A lead that has moved by under a sixteenth of `d0` is kept, so noise in the velocity does not make the selection look again.
- **The loader works in classes** (B1 of the scheduling research): the bricks the bands split first (they are drawn coarser until their children arrive: a soft miss), then the margin, then prefetch, and by distance from the camera in each. The view direction orders nothing: the loader is not told it, and the selection does not look at it, so a turn needs nothing the scene did not already have. Work that nothing wants any more is dropped when a worker comes to it.
- **Soft misses are limited by generation, not by residency** (#143). On the 50 m/s flights the loader is several times slower than what the camera asks for, so margins and prefetch reduce misses and do not remove them. ADR 0009 (speed-scaled LOD) is the answer to that.
- **Eviction** is distance-band first, then LRU inside the band, with 10–20% overflow and no eviction of the guard, the current frame's set, the margin, in-flight work or a live Edit chain (#141, #143). Prefetch units are not kept: they are candidates like any other, and go first when the store is over its budget since they were never stamped as wanted. It runs on the scene thread (ADR 0006), a part of the units at a time.
- **A growth's peak is not in either size** (#145). A scene buffer that fills grows alone, and while it does the old buffer and the new one both exist on the GPU, and the frame thread's copy may hold both allocations of the `Vec` it lengthens. The sizes below are of the buffers after a growth; the real-time bench reports the peak during one (`memory.scene_gpu_peak_growth_mib`, `memory.scene_host_peak_growth_mib`), and does not gate it: the `scene_mib` gate is on the words in use.
- **Two sizes are budgeted, and they are different quantities.** The drawn scene is bounded by the bands. The host store holds the bricks the scene splits, their ancestors, the margin's children and what the bands do not draw, except the air and the buried bricks, which the loader does not generate (#142); the budget is the complete shell with the margins, with a 10% overflow before eviction starts. The prefetch simulator's shell stops at the horizon, and so does the client's since the horizon cap (#146).
- **The horizon cap** (#146): nothing past the Planet's horizon can be seen, so the bands do not split, and the residency does not want, a brick that no place the camera can be seen from could see a part of. The Planet's sea level is a sphere of radius `R` (`render::lod::Horizon`; the ground is nowhere under it, and the sea is a Material, so the sphere is an occluder), and two points `c` and `r` from the centre see each other only if their distance is at most `sqrt(c² − R²) + sqrt(r² − R²)`, the length of the line that grazes the sphere. A brick is in the cap if the distance from the camera raised by the vertical margin to its box is within that for the box's farthest corner `r`: so a peak or a ridge is seen from beyond the sea's horizon (a brick's own height is in it), and the test is exact for a sphere, not an approximation of it. What is out is not a hole: it stays whole at the coarse level that holds it, which is what makes the cap a limit on refinement and not on what is drawn; a brick that is out of it is out of every level's bands, and the Guard is resident whatever the cap says (so what is past the horizon is the Guard's levels and the coarse bricks over what is split, a skin of the Planet at the Guard's level, not nothing). Residency is the same test seen from the places the radial margin (1.25×) and the lead (1 s and 2 s) look from. A brick that is in is held in until the camera is a twentieth further, as the bands' boundaries are. The cap is off while the raised camera is under the sphere (under the sea, or dug in: nothing then shadows what is seen) and comes back on 50 m over it, so that a swimmer at the surface does not turn it on and off, which looks at every node. **Not covered:** the caves below sea level, past the horizon of a camera in a cave (a cave's far interior is coarse, as before the cap it was fine); and the transparent water of M1b, which will need an occluder lower than sea level. **Open:** at 1.25× the vertical take-off, which pushes the horizon out a hundred metres for each metre it climbs, has soft misses with the cap that it did not have without it (218 of 359 frames, at most 2 756 bricks: `docs/measurements.md`, the horizon cap's entry); a reach of 1.75× over the horizon for residency clears them and holds 4% more bricks. The margin is the dev's call and is not changed here. A frustum cull is a debug mode only, as the ADR says: the cap is the only culling that the game does.
- **At the cap, the LOD is coarsened** (#143): when eviction has taken what it can (everything left is wanted) and the store is over its limit, the farthest bands are drawn at a larger bias, a step at a time: levels 8 and up at a bias of 1.5, then 7 and up, 6, 5, then 4 and up at 2, then 2 and up at 2 (`Bands::coarsened`, `COARSENING` in `scene_worker`). A step selects and packs the scene again (as a change of bias does), at most one in 10 s, and the units the coarser bands do not want go to eviction. A step is taken back by what the scene wants, not by what the store holds (eviction stops once the store is under its budget, so a rule on its size would never take a step back): at each step the units the store held for a node of the selection are recorded, and a step is taken back, once the last has stood for its hold and nothing is on its way, when the nodes the scene wants now, scaled by the bands' `Bands::weight` at the step before over that now, and by that units-per-node, are under 95% of the budget. A step is taken at the limit (up to 10% over the budget), so there is a gap of 15% or more between taking a step and taking it back, as well as the hold, and the steps do not flip. The steps are coarse, so the budget should be sized so that the shell fits.
- The shell is the precondition for the horizon cap and the LOD claims: culling without a complete shell is a hole, and LOD without prefetch is a pop.
- Most of the price is paid in M1a-2, not M1a-1: the walk slice ships a simpler ring loader, and the shell lands with flight and orbit. The guard is the exception (2026-10-31): the walk slice's loader (#86) generates it right after the spawn ring, so nothing beyond the spawn ring is ever sky.
