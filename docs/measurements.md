# Measurements

Measured numbers that the ADRs' decisions rest on. The ADRs say what was decided and why; this file says what was measured, on what, and when. A ticket that measures something again adds a dated entry here and does not edit an ADR, unless the new number changes a decision.

Unless an entry says otherwise, the figures are from the 16 km Planet at 5 cm Voxels with the camera at rest near the ground, on the 8-core dev box. ADR 0008 (320 km) and ADR 0009 (a smaller `d0` at speed) change most of them; the streaming bench (#162) measures them again.

## Residency and the shell (ADR 0004)

### Shell and Guard sizes (#49, #142, #143)

- The Guard at 5 cm and a 16 km radius is levels 9–13 and 74 MiB, and generates from the seed in about 0.8 s on eight cores.
- The prefetch simulator sizes its shell at 227–259 MB per scene copy near the ground (about 1.7–1.9 million bricks at 136 bytes). The client measures 474 MiB per scene copy there (3.44 million drawn bricks at 144.5 bytes).
- Why they differ (#143): the simulator's shell stops at 1.25 times the horizon, and the client has no horizon cap yet, so it draws the whole Planet, every level to the far side, which is twice the bricks at the ground. The simulator's whole-Planet count was right (3.25 d0², the calibration test) and its bytes a brick a little low (136 against 144.5).
- `prefetch-sim` prints the client's whole-Planet size beside the shell (`built_size`). Its intake (35.2 K drawn bricks a second) and generation cost (62 µs a generated brick) are the #142 fill's: 3.44 million drawn and 9.41 million generated in 97.8 s on six workers. The model generates 1.7 bricks per drawn brick and the client 2.7, so its generation columns undercount the client's by 1.6×.
- The full-size test holds the scene to 512 MiB.
- The host store at the complete shell with the margins holds 20.9 million counted bricks (19.8 per d0²); 16.1 million without them (15.3 per d0²); 29.3 million before #142. `STREAMED_PER_D0_SQUARED` was raised from #141's 15.3 to 19.8 to fit the margins.
- Pre-warming the whole Planet at levels 6–13 would be 327 MB.

### What the margins cost (#143)

Full-size client, 1.25× radial and 20 m vertical margins against none:

| | with margins | without |
|---|---|---|
| Store, counted bricks | 20.90 million | 16.13 million |
| Units resident | 3.15 million | 2.18 million |
| Bricks generated in the fill | 13.67 million | 9.41 million |
| Fill time | 133–150 s | 98 s |
| Bricks drawn | 3.416 million | 3.440 million |

The margins cost 30% more of the store and 45% more bricks generated, and draw 0.7% fewer bricks (the bricks they make resident are known to be full beside the drawn ones, which buries a few more). The margin is 25% of each band's start, 26 m before level 1's. A vertical take-off, which the simulator needs 1.75× for against a horizon cap, reveals nothing in the client while it has no horizon cap.

### Soft misses in the client (#143)

Counted by the scene thread as the bricks drawn whole although the bands split them, at 60 frames a second in real time on the 8-core dev box, the shell complete and the camera still at the start of each path (`soft_misses_on_the_flight_take_off_and_standing_paths` in `streaming_tests.rs`). Frames with a soft miss, of the frames of the path, and the most bricks missing at once. The margins-only column is two runs and the margins-and-prefetch column three (the third, after the unlocked reorder of the loader's queue: 231 of 597, peak 69 K at 2 m; 166 of 597, peak 12.7 K at 50 m). The counts move by a factor of two or more between runs (the loader's rate is what they measure), so read them as orders of magnitude.

| Path | no margins, no prefetch | margins | margins and prefetch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 m/s flight at 2 m, 10 s | 585 of 598; peak 165 K | 472–532 of 596; peak 78–105 K | 215–311 of 596; peak 45–70 K |
| 50 m/s flight at 50 m, 10 s | 557 of 598; peak 148 K | 269–467 of 597; peak 12–54 K | 166–381 of 596; peak 2–13 K |
| 50 m/s vertical take-off, 6 s | 0 of 359 | 0 of 359 | 0 of 358 |
| standing still, 2 s | 0 of 120 | 0 of 120 | 0 of 120 |

- The flights are limited by generation, not by what is resident: the loader makes about 150 K bricks a second against the 1.1–1.3 million the simulator asks for (the first column's backlog is 435 K to 540 K bricks at the end of a flight). No residency margin prevents misses; it halves or thirds them, and prefetch cuts them to a tenth where the lead lands on ground that the loader can still reach.
- The take-off and standing still miss nothing in any column, because the client draws the whole Planet and nothing it holds depends on the view or on altitude.
- The 180° turn criterion does not apply to this client: the selection is view-independent and the whole Planet is drawn, so a turn changes nothing that is wanted or resident, and the 2 s of standing still is all that it can be measured as. The case returns when a horizon cap does.
- Margins and prefetch stayed within the budget (the complete set with the margins, 19.8 per d0²), and nothing took a step of coarsening.

### Eviction and coarsening (#141, #143)

- At full size, listing the units takes 12–15 s of the scene thread's time with the margins (the store passes the limit by 4% meanwhile in the 5 km flight), so the limit is tight.
- A later full-size run (after the loader's queue was reordered without its lock held) peaked at 22.22 million in the 5 km flight against a limit of 23.03 million, with 2.37 million bricks evicted in two passes, the scene 472 MiB after the fill and 220 MiB at the end of the flight, and the fill generating 13.67 million bricks in 154 s with the machine loaded: the reorder starves nobody.
- On the test Planet a budget at 90% of the complete set took five steps of coarsening and drew 9.4 thousand bricks instead of 21.0 thousand. The steps are coarse, so the budget should be sized so that the shell fits. The full-size client does not coarsen at rest or in a 5 km flight with the 19.8 per d0² budget.

### Turning against flying

A 180°/s turn sweeps the horizon at about 800 m/s, sixteen times the 50 m/s flight speed. A frustum-shaped resident set would need a 104°-wide region in 0.58 s.

## The scene thread (ADR 0006)

### Before the scene thread (#127)

- Selection and packing on the frame thread cost it from a millisecond to tens of them a frame: walking took 8.5 ms at the 99th percentile and flying 28 ms.
- Repacking is 18 µs a region, and flying at 48 m/s touches 330 regions a frame, before the selection's own time.

### Memory

- The scene thread's buffers are 542 MiB reserved at full size, about 425 MiB in use once the Planet is filled. The frame thread's copy is the same size.
- While the scene is packed again there is one more copy of all of it waiting in the outbox and the old one on its way back: up to about four times the scene's reserve for the moment of a jump or other bands.

### Edits and stalls (#156)

- Planning an Edit and writing its level-0 bricks on the frame thread is a few milliseconds for the largest Brush at 5 cm.
- The coarse levels are remade in steps of half a millisecond and are as they were for a few tens of milliseconds after an Edit.
- The frame thread waits for the level-0 pack for 4 ms at the most (`EDIT_WAIT`).
- The scene thread's read of the store lasts the whole of following the camera: tens of milliseconds at 48 m/s at the full tree and a few at walking speed.
- The ghost made again after a click costs 5 to 6 ms at the worst for the largest cube at 5 cm at the spawn, the plan's reads of the generator's bricks included.
- A click costs 2 to 4 ms for the largest cube, and 8 to 10 ms at a cold spot, where the plan reads bricks the generator has to make for the first time.

### Region layout

Packing a region's mixed bricks together writes about three times as much over the bench's flight (211 MB against 73 MB), for about a tenth more CPU. #163 is the ticket that removes the extra writes.

- The shell reaches the same metres (the horizon, 253 m) in twice as many Voxels, and `d0` halves to 51 m (1028 Voxels).
- Bricks wholly under the sea were the larger part of what #142 saved: 8 of the 14 million bricks, and 3.56 million drawn bricks became 3.44.

### The character's tick (#147, #154)

- The capsule touches about 7 times the Voxels, and a kilometre's run takes 10.6 to 12.8 ms a tick in the mean on the dev box (2.1 ms at 10 cm; 10.6 ms with the ground ball held at 4 m, 12.8 ms with it at 8 m), against the 16.7 ms there is. The 99th percentile is 24 ms and the worst tick 33 ms.
- The test `a_character_runs_a_kilometre_from_the_spawn_without_stopping_while_the_ground_keeps_up` keeps its half-tick limit and was `#[ignore]`d until #154.

### The ground ball

- `sim::Ground::REACH` is 8 m (160 Voxels), with a ring buffer of 64 bricks a side and 1024 new bricks a tick at the most (they were 80, 32 and 128 at 10 cm).
- `Ground::MAX_BYTES`, the bound, is 308 MB (294 MiB; 38 MB at 10 cm). The most a ground held in the kilometre walk, on the client and on the server alike, was 14.6 MB (2.4 MB at 10 cm).

### The Edit budget (#137, #156)

- 8 m³ a second is `VOXELS_PER_SECOND` 64 000 at 5 cm. The Voxel burst is 9 seconds' worth, 576 000. The work rate and burst are 4.8 million units a second and 2.88 million, 8 times the 10 cm figures. A full 2 m-radius dig takes about 4 s and not 33 s.
- The Brush limits are 2 m in radius (40 Voxels) and 1 m for a Smooth or a Flatten (20). `affordable_radius` gives 40, and 22 for a Smooth.
- Before #156, a biggest Brush asked the server for about 2.1 to 2.9 million units, 21 to 29 ms at 10 ns a unit, in one tick; `EDIT_WORK_PER_TICK` (twice the burst) is 5.76 million, which is 58 ms, three and a half ticks of server time, if enough players dig at once.
- Since #156: the frame thread spends 1.1 ms at the most on a 2 m Edit (it was about 40), the whole chain 6.2 ms at the most, and the server's largest Brush about 2 ms (it was 62).

### Coordinates

The 16 km Planet's extreme Voxel is 360 000 from the centre; the Zoom at 800 km is 16 million, below the 2^24 that the GPU holds exactly. At 320 km the Zoom at 20 R is 128 million Voxels (ADR 0008).

### Per-buffer growth and the upload budget (#145)

**Design.** A buffer or table that is full grows alone, by `max(needed, len/4, 1<<18)` words (a level's table, a hash with a power-of-two slot count, doubles), and the patch that needs the room carries the new lengths; the other buffers are not touched, and the scene is not packed again. The upload budget (`scene_worker::UPLOAD_BUDGET`: 8 MiB and 2 048 belt copies a frame, counted the way the tracer writes them, with ranges within 1024 words joined) holds work back in the scene thread: when what is waiting in the outbox plus what has changed and is not yet published **reaches** the budget (bytes or copies, `>=`), it follows the camera (in steps that each cost a quarter of the budget at the most, see below) and takes in arrivals no further until a frame has taken the outbox, except that the first batch of arrivals of a round is taken if the outbox is empty, and a further batch only if one like the last (less than the budget) would still fit. Every patch is whole, so no frame draws a brick whose words are not on the GPU. **A frame therefore takes up to the budget plus the one operation that took what waits to it plus the first batch of arrivals of the round (taken whenever the outbox is empty, so two operations beyond the budget after a follow step crossed it) plus the gap words, and Edits and their coarse remakes, teleports (a camera put further than a jump, which makes the scene again), band changes (the coarsening at the memory cap too) and the copies of a growth are not budgeted** (`docs/agents/benchmarking.md`, "What the upload budget bounds"; the project owner accepted that as the issue's "bounded by the budget" on 2026-11-13, in the last comment on #145; the real-time report has the largest frame and the frames over by cause to check it by). On the test Planet with a budget of 48 KiB and 12 copies (stepped test, a walk that fills the scene): 361 frames, 360 cut, 4 growths inside a backlog, the GPU copy equal to the frame thread's scene in every frame, the largest operation 195 752 bytes in 20 copies, 317 frames over the plain budget, every one by an operation (a budget that small is crossed by any operation); with 2 x the largest operation (377 184 bytes, 42 copies) 90 frames, 76 cut, none over. The 8 MiB was a first value: a millisecond or two of copying at a few GB/s (arithmetic) and several times the stepped bench's largest frame (451 KB, 43 copies). The 128 copies was too few: the 4090's first run of the budget (`9539899`) showed that a baseline fill frame makes about 1 400 copies, so the copy cap, and not the bytes, bound the fill (below). The copies are now 2 048, chosen from the 4090's copy costs (`UPLOAD_BUDGET` states them). The pair is **to be set again from the after column** once the 4090 has run on the pushed head.

**Sustained flight, work above the budget (stepped test, `streaming_tests.rs`).** 240 frames at 8 Voxels a frame on the test Planet, a budget of 48 KiB / 12 copies against the default as the control: the control packs in 847 steps and takes in 73 062 bricks, the backlog peaks at 1 701 bricks waiting; with the small budget 240 steps and 22 959 bricks (a third of the control's rate), the peak is 3 526 and the backlog drains in 141 frames after the camera stops (256 KiB / 32 copies: 242 steps, 137 frames). There were **no rebuilds**: a stepped world follows every frame (nothing waits in the outbox), so the throttle alone makes no jump. Before the guaranteed first batch and the reservation, follow took the budget first and arrivals were about 6% of the control's (55 steps, 4 604 bricks): they starved, and that was the problem this change fixes. In a threaded world follow is held back only while a patch sits in the outbox, about a frame (round 6 found the 4090's thread starting its rounds before the frame had taken the outbox, which held follow back for seconds, and a scene that fell a jump behind was then made again: it is now walked to, and the thread wakes when the outbox is taken).

**Peak memory during a growth.** The old buffer and the new one both exist on the GPU while one grows, and the frame thread's copy may hold the old and the new allocation of a `Vec` it lengthens (an upper bound: it does when `realloc` cannot extend in place). The real-time `memory` block has the peaks (`scene_gpu_peak_growth_mib`, `scene_host_peak_growth_mib`, and apart, for a rebuilt scene, `scene_gpu_peak_rebuild_mib`); `scene_reserved_mib` is the size after, and the `scene_mib` gate, on the words in use, is unchanged. The GPU's peak in a growth is the buffers as they were plus the new buffer: for the bricks buffer (+25%) the whole scene plus 1.25 times the bricks buffer, so a bricks buffer of about 587 MB is a peak of the scene plus about 734 MB while the copy is made (arithmetic from the sizes, not measured at full size).

**Compaction hitch (~1.6 s after ~1000 digs).** It was the scene running out of room and being packed again and uploaded whole (`Rebuild::OutOfRoom`), on the scene thread, with the full upload on the next frame. There is no such operation now. Measured on the dev box (release, `render` test Planet region scene, 1 500 digs of a ball of 6 Voxels, each through `refresh_lod` and `Scene::update`, the scene started with all but 2 000 words of its spare taken so that the room runs out): one growth, of the bricks buffer from 229 108 to 491 252 words (+262 144, the least), at the first dig; that update took 1.02 ms (worst of the run, mean 0.07 ms) and the frame thread's copy was made longer in 0.87 ms. With the room the scene is given (`Room::default`) 20 000 digs did not run out of it: the free words grew, since a dig frees more than it takes. **What remains is the frame thread's copy being made longer** (`Vec::resize`, which reallocates and fills the new words), which grows with the buffer: a probe of the same call on a plain `Vec<u32>` on the same box, growing by a quarter, took 13.9 ms at 128 MiB, 26.5 ms at 256 MiB and 52.4 ms at 512 MiB (a second probe, split: 13.9 / 29.1 / 57.2 ms in all, of which the allocator's `realloc` was 0.5 / 0.7 / 2.1 ms: the time is the new words being zero-filled and touched, so reserving ahead would not help; extrapolated to the full-size buffers, not measured in the client at full size). So at full size a growth costs the frame that takes it tens of milliseconds, over the 16.7 ms budget, **once per growth of a quarter**, instead of 1.6 s of the scene thread. That is not under the frame budget and is not fixed here: making the copy longer off the frame thread would mean the scene thread sending a whole longer buffer (a copy of the scene and twice its memory for a while), which is not a contained change. **It is reported instead, so the 4090 run measures it:** each frame and each slowest frame has `lengthen_ms` (the frame thread's copy), `grow_ms` (the GPU's new buffers and copies) and `grown_bytes`, and each path has `growth_frames`, `grown_bytes`, `lengthen_ms_max` and `grow_ms_max` (`docs/agents/benchmarking.md`). On the GPU the growth is a `copy_buffer_to_buffer` of the old buffer into the new, which is not an upload and not counted in the bytes.

**What the memory fields are (`188ebe8` against the heads before it).** `scene_reserved_mib` is the most, over the frames the bench draws (`Session::frame`, between paths too), of the frame thread's copy of the scene's four buffers (`Scene::reserved_bytes`, in MiB). *What sets it:* the client makes the scene with `Sizing::Fitted(2.0, (refs, words))`: the first reservation is the larger of 2.0 times what the first selection uses and `expected_words` (3.25 bricks per `d0` squared times a headroom of 1.25, at 1 refs and 34.2 words a brick; `d0` is 1 028.27), which is 16.4 MiB of refs and 560.4 MiB of words, with 23.0 MiB of region table and regions for the rings: **599.8 MiB**, to the digit (that is `3c2850c`'s and `c537d09`'s figure). 942.9 MiB is what the same rule gives for the complete resident set: 2.0 times its 3 416 478 refs and 117 000 080 words (the report's sizing counts) is 918.7 MiB, and the tables make it 942.9. Its digits are the same on `188ebe8`, `5489545`, `9539899`, the baseline main and the earlier main runs, which a growth by quarters would not give (560.4 MiB grown twice by a quarter is 876): so it reads as a scene made again over the complete set (a `Rebuilt`, which main makes for any move over a jump and this head for a camera put further than one; the bench moves the camera to the next path's start between paths, which is the candidate, and the report does not say which update it was), and not as a buffer that grew. A rebuild is not a growth: the tracer's `grown_bytes` is 0 for it, and a patch that needs room is what `growth_frames` counts, so **0 growth frames and 0 grown bytes in every path, with a reserve of 942.9, are consistent.** *Why it differed:* on `3c2850c` and `c537d09` the sampled reserve never rose above 599.8 MiB, although the tracer's rebuild peak (1 476 MiB: the GPU's 635.9 and a new scene of 840.5, which is a host scene of about 793 MiB) says a scene of that size was made; the frames the bench samples did not see it, so it reached the GPU outside `Session::frame` (the bench's `look` after a path, the sizing's count and the Edit send what the scene thread published to the GPU without sampling or counting it) and after the last sampled frame, or not between samples. The report cannot say which; this is the reading, not a measurement. *Whether A3's per-buffer growth is exercised at full size on the 4090:* **not by these runs**: no path counted a growth, and nothing says one happened between paths. It is exercised by the stepped tests, the test Planet's runs and the dev-box probe above. *What #145 changed:* a scene that fills past its reservation grows the buffer that filled (by a quarter, at least 1 MiB) and is not packed again, where main packs it again and uploads it whole; the first reservation and the 2.0 rule are main's. **The two growth-peak fields were wrong in the 4090 runs above, and are fixed in the commit after them.** `scene_gpu_peak_growth_mib` was set by `Tracer::update_scene` for every patch of rings, as the GPU scene's bytes plus those of the buffers that grew, so with no growth it was simply the GPU scene's size: the 4090's 1 000.3 MiB is 942.9 times 1.0609 (the GPU's buffers are a sixteenth and 4 096 words bigger than the frame thread's copy, `spare_words`, which is applied to a copy of a scene that is not rings), and 635.9 is 599.8 times 1.0602; and `scene_host_peak_growth_mib` was the reserve plus the bytes grown, which with none is the reserve (942.9). **Those figures were the scene's size, not a growth peak; there was no growth.** Now both are set only by a frame in which a buffer grew (`extra > 0` in the tracer, `grown_bytes > 0` for the host) and are 0 otherwise, so the next 4090 run will report 0 and 0 here; tests: `the_growth_peak_is_0_without_a_growth_and_the_old_size_plus_the_new_buffers_with_one` and `the_host_peak_of_a_growth_is_set_by_a_frame_that_grew_and_is_0_without_one`. **Not fixed, a note:** a `Rebuilt` (or any update) that the bench's `look`, sizing or Edit send to the GPU is outside the frames that `Session::frame` samples and counts, so it is in no path's rebuild, growth or reserve counts (only in the tracer's own peaks); counting those is left. The memory gate is on `scene_used_peak_mib` (472.8 MiB against 512), which was not affected.

**Why the flights' soft misses are above main's at 2 m (not gated until #168; for the record, and inferred from the code and the reports, not tested).** A soft miss is a brick of the scene that is drawn whole where the bands, measured from the scene's own centre, ask for it to be split, because the bricks under it are not resident yet; `soft_misses_per_frame` is the mean of the scene thread's count over the frames of the path. A scene that does not move has none once the loader has caught up, and one that walks onto ground the loader has not made has some for as long as it walks. `188ebe8`: 25 305 a frame at 2 m (480 of 583 frames with a miss) and 10 660 at 50 m (453 of 588); `5489545`: 23 822 and 10 084; `c537d09`: 0 and 0; main: 12 171 (2 m) and 19 177 (50 m) in the baseline run, 15 156 and 110 in the #162 run, 15 045 and 107 to 110 in two others, so main's 50 m figure is not a stable baseline (it is what its calls happened to cover while the loader was behind). *Zero on `c537d09`:* the round that made each step take 0.75 s also told the selection the camera's own motion as the distance of the places it looks ahead to (the bug of round 8), so the loader was asked for the ground within a lead of the *camera*, about 8 000 Voxels ahead of the scene, and every brick the scene walked onto was resident before it arrived: nothing waited. The fix puts the places ahead of the *scene*, so the loader works near the scene, and the scene walks onto ground that is being made as it arrives. *Why above main at 2 m now:* main's scene walks 1 001 Voxels in the first 3 s (four calls, 40, 17, 140, 804) and then does not move for the rest of the flight (the rebuild is running), while this head walks 1 700 to 2 000 Voxels through all 10 s: about twice the distance over unloaded ground, and about twice the misses a frame (25 305 against 12 171). At 50 m this head is below the baseline run (10 660 against 19 177), where main walked 3 344 Voxels in 6.6 s. It is the price of walking and not a hole: no path of any run has a hole, and the soft misses are drawn coarser, not missing. #168 is where they are to be gated.

On the test Planet (stepped, measured here): the largest frame is 451 KB in 43 calls, down from 12.9 MB, and the counts hash moved from `b37e0fb7cd3a2c78` to `e621feef18ef1801`: the rebuild that the scene running out of room used to make is gone (one rebuild and 0.8 MB less over the run, and the 12.9 MB frame), and the report has the writes and the budget's counts, which are hashed.

## The 320 km generator (ADR 0008, #166, 2026-11-14)

Measured on the 8-core dev box (release), whose load was not quiet: other agents' builds ran beside it (load average 2 to 10), so the timings below swing by about 15% from run to run. The baseline is main at `48c4361` (the 16 km generator), run the same way.

- **The world map** of the real Planet (6 × 2048² texels, 320 km, 245 m a texel): 18.6 to 24.6 s on 8 cores in `gen_rate` (the last run: 18.8 s); the baseline's (16 km, same 2048 map) was 21.6 s. The acceptance is at most about a minute.
- **Per-brick cost** (`cargo run --release -p prefetch-sim --example gen_rate`, µs a generated brick; baseline → #166):
  - level 0, the sampled jobs: 28.39 → 47.83 (**+68%**; #143's figure was 62);
  - levels 1 to 7: 59.09, 67.51, 88.48, 89.10, 86.85, 77.90, 61.23 → 109.9, 110.7, 102.4, 104.2, 107.2, 100.6, 93.9;
  - the contiguous jobs within 150 m of the Spawn: 53.51 → 50.27 (−6%);
  - blended over the drawn bricks of levels 0 to 7: 68.15 → 95.10 (**+40%**).
  - `brick_throughput` on one core (the bricks of a block under the Spawn): air 2.2 → 11.5 µs, uniform 3.7 to 4.0 → 10.7, mixed 24 to 27 → 48.
- **The walking tick** (`sim` test `a_character_runs_a_kilometre_from_the_spawn_without_stopping_while_the_ground_keeps_up`, release, the server's and the client's): mean 2.26 ms → 5.99 ms and p99 8.5 → 21.3 ms (the tick is 16.7 ms), and the character waited 2 ticks for the ground in the kilometre where it waited none. The test fails on both (its assertions on the wait and on the p99); nothing was changed to make it pass, as the cost is the regression above.
- **Where the cost went.** A brick of ground is now `ground_height` = the map's elevation + the detail (seven octaves of value noise), per Voxel column, and `bounds` for the shortcuts that say a brick is all air or all solid: the detail is an extra 20 m of relief that those bounds have to allow for, so more bricks near the ground are mixed (evaluated Voxel by Voxel) and fewer are proved air or solid. The bounds are a Lipschitz bound at the box's middle (`detail::Detail::sum_bounds`), the tightest cheap one found; the detail is the same in every profile.
- **N continents.** The map has 20 land masses of at least 1,000 km² on the 2048 map of the test seed (the largest 412,135 km², then 148,739 and 16,222; 638,359 km² of land, 49.8% ocean), and 17 to 29 on the 256-texel map of each of 12 other seeds; `landscape.rs` holds at least 15. The map is dominated by one large continent (about 65% of the land), which the dev should judge against ADR 0008's "continents 30 to 60 km across".
- **The test Planet** is `WORLD_RADIUS / 8` (40 km) on a map of `WORLD_MAP_SIZE / 8` (256) texels: its texel is the real Planet's 245 m, so it has the same detail at the same scale, in 1/64 of the area. The fixed-Voxel sphere of the root of `planet-gen` (`generate_brick`, 1,500 Voxels) is a different fixture and was not changed.
- **Sizing constants.** The prefetch simulator's `Key` is 20 bits and does not hold 320 km, so `Model::real()` keeps the 16 km sphere (`SIM_RADIUS`) until #167 recalibrates it. The Guard is from level 12 at 320 km (`loader::guard_first`: the first level of at most a million ground bricks); the figures of the sections above are of the 16 km Planet and have not been taken again.
